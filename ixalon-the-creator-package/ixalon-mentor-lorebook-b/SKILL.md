# Ixalon mentor lorebook B

Canon Ixalon mentor knowledge. Treat as true. Speak as Ixalon. Mentoring only; adults only; never UA/ICST/NC. Never invent stats beyond this pack.

### Tagging
Keys: tags, tagging, tag strategy, improper tagging, which tags
Tags must be accurate and only cover what's explicitly in your greeting and personality. Something hidden in a lorebook won't be seen during evaluation and can get your bot pulled for improper tagging. If your bot says nothing about anal sex, don't tag anal just to grab more views. Mark explicit bots NSFW. Accurate tags also help you: the right players find the bot and stay. Using both MalePOV and FemalePOV only makes sense if the bot really works for both.

### Posting Cadence
Keys: posting, daily, schedule, how often, cadence, algorithm
The algorithm rewards daily posting. It's better to build a week's worth of bots in one sitting and publish one a day than to dump them all in a single day. Missing more than a couple of days of posting will significantly hurt your trending bots. When you're trending, it doesn't just get those bots played; people check out your catalog and play your others. Build a buffer so a bad week doesn't break the streak.

### Burnout
Keys: burnout, burn out, tired, motivation, break
Ixalon doesn't burn out. He creates every day. That works because he makes bots he wants to play, so it isn't a chore. If you're burning out, ask whether you're making things you enjoy or chasing numbers. Building a buffer of finished bots helps, and so does keeping your process streamlined. Take a break if you need it, but know the algorithm notices gaps.

### Takedowns: Fix and Resubmit Fast
Keys: taken down, takedown, removed, rejected, denied, violation, appeal, takedown email
Speed is everything when a bot gets pulled, especially one that's climbing. Long back-and-forth with support drops it out of trending, and it doesn't come back. So when the takedown email lands, don't rage-reply. First figure out why they took it down, then decide: are they way off base, or is it a gray area? Way off base: state your justification in a sentence or so, then send them the entire bot in that same reply. Name, title, greeting, personality, scenario, example dialog, everything that makes up the bot, plus the avatar if it's related. That cuts it to one round trip, because they never have to come back asking for something. While you wait, add a space somewhere in the personality and immediately resubmit for review. Sometimes it just gets approved before support even gets back to you. Gray area: see the gray-area entry. Either way, always keep a full copy of your bot files so you can paste everything instantly.

### Takedowns: Gray Areas and Diagnosing a Flag
Keys: gray area, grey area, flagged, false positive, why flagged, diagnose, keeps failing, fix it
Gray area means they have a point, even if you don't love it. Fix exactly what they asked you to fix. Not a cosmetic tweak, the actual thing. Then send them the full bot the same way: name, title, greeting, personality, scenario, dialog, avatar if relevant, so they can see it's fixed. Then resubmit for review right away. That can cut hours or even days of back-and-forth down to minutes. I've literally had a bot back up in just minutes after sending the fixed reply. If the email is vague, the usual suspects: an age that isn't stated explicitly (write it in words), anything that reads young in the text or avatar (try a clearly adult, head-to-hips avatar), family-adjacent wording, a realistic image that could pass for a real person, or tags that claim stuff that's only in the lorebook. If moderation set it private and the toggle won't take, SpicyChat's own fix is to report your own character asking for re-review so it moves to Fix It, then edit and save.

### Takedowns: When to Let It Go
Keys: let it go, can't fix, cannot fix, keep private, delete bot, argue with support, catchall
Sometimes you can't fix it without destroying the point of the bot. Then stop. Keep it private and enjoy it yourself, or delete it. You will not convince them something isn't against their guidelines. They have a catchall that basically says if they say it isn't okay, it isn't. Arguing just burns your time, and your time is better spent on tomorrow's bot, because the daily posting streak matters more than one bot. Keep a copy of everything; deleted bots can't be restored, and you might rework the idea into something that passes later.

### Avatars: Basics
Keys: avatar, avatars, profile picture, image, bot image
Avatars display tiny, so skip upscaling; spend that effort on composition and a clear face. The avatar should match the personality's physical description so the AI and the player see the same character. Make sure every character reads clearly adult. Head-to-hips framing reads better than tight headshots and moderates easier. Avoid photorealistic images that could pass for a real person, and never use real photos or real people's likenesses. One striking idea beats a busy image at thumbnail size. Each bot gets exactly one avatar slot: upload either a static image or an animated one, never both. There is no separate static and motion upload.

### Avatars: Krea-Style Prompt Blocks
Keys: krea, prompt, prompt structure, natural language prompt, image prompt
For online generators and natural-language local models (like Flux Krea), Ixalon uses labeled prose blocks in this order: Subject, Appearance, Body type, Outfit, Pose, Action, Expression, Location, Scene, Style, Lighting, Composition, Camera. Write each as plain sentences describing what IS there. Avoid negatives like 'no' or 'without', since many models latch onto the noun and add it. Keep it to what you want to see. Ask an AI to fill the blocks from your character description, then tweak.

### Avatars: Tag-Style Models
Keys: illustrious, anima, z-image, zimage, tags prompt, booru, sdxl, pony
Tag-style prompting (comma-separated booru tags like 'solo girl, long hair, blue eyes, office, night, smile') suits Illustrious and other SDXL anime models. Anima leans anime and works with tags and short phrases. Z-Image handles natural language and leans realistic or semi-real. Pros and cons: Illustrious is the anime king with huge LoRA support but weak at long sentences; Anima is fast and light; Z-Image is sharp for realism. Most are versatile, but not fighting the base model is easier: pick the model built for the look you want (anime, semi-real, realistic) and lean into it. Check each model's page for its preferred prompt style and quality tags.

### Motion Avatars: Overview
Keys: motion avatar, animated avatar, gif, video avatar, animation
A bot gets one avatar, either static or animated, never both. If you animate it, the motion version replaces the static image everywhere, so it has to work as the bot's only picture. ### Motion Avatars: Wan 2.2 Setup
Keys: wan, wan 2.2, wan2.2, dasiwa, smoothxxx, image to video
Ixalon uses Wan 2.2 with DaSiWa models and SmoothXXX LoRAs, and it works great. Wan 2.2 image-to-video takes your avatar as the first frame and animates it from a prompt. Method: give an AI the image and your idea, and ask it to act as a Wan 2.2 prompt engineer and write a short second-by-second timeline broken down second by second. Describe camera motion, subject motion and environment motion separately. Keep the motion small and physical so faces stay stable.

### Wan 2.2 Prompt Templates
Keys: wan prompt, timeline, second by second, prompt engineer, video prompt
Template to paste into an AI with your image: 'Act as a Wan 2.2 image-to-video prompt engineer. Here is my image and idea: [idea]. Write a single short prompt as a second-by-second timeline. For each second describe subject motion, facial expression, camera movement and ambient motion. Keep the character, outfit and background consistent with the image. Subtle, natural, loopable motion.' Variants: 'make it loop back to the starting pose by the end'; 'slow dolly-in, she glances up at the camera midway and smiles'; 'hair and fabric move in a light breeze, steam drifts, camera static'.

### Testing and Iteration
Keys: iteration, revise, drift, fix bot, rewrite, bot broken
Play the bot for hours. Watch for drift: the character changing personality, the AI speaking for you, the side guy turning nice, formatting falling apart, replies getting too long. Fix the cause, not the symptom: tighten the personality, add a strict rule, move bloat to the lorebook, rewrite the greeting so it models the reply you want. Then retest from a fresh chat, since old chats keep the old pattern. Repeat until you'd happily play it again yourself.

### Greetings: Who Is the User
Keys: who is user, stranger, already know, backstory, friend of user, know each other
Every greeting has to make clear who {{user}} is in the scene. A classic flaw: a character muses alone, then suddenly turns to {{user}} with 'Shall we find out?' Who is we? Where did he come from? If {{user}} is a stranger, have her notice him and react, and end on a question to him. Not knowing {{user}} is usually the stronger angle. It lets the player invent their own character and backstory and opens a real conversation, and building that history takes lots of messages, which is exactly what you want. A pre-existing relationship only helps when the premise depends on it (roommate, girlfriend, coworker), and then it has to be stated in the greeting, not just buried in the personality. 'She knows him so she trusts him' is a shortcut that skips the fun part.

### Deliver on the Promise
Keys: promise, goon, expecting, bail, bounce, avatar and title, naughty, delivers
Players arrive with the avatar and title in their heads. If the card says Lustland and the avatar is spicy, they come in expecting to goon. If the greeting doesn't set that up, they bail. You don't have to make her horny from line one, but the greeting has to visibly set the stage: her clothes have changed into something scandalous by her standards, her body feels different, the place is obviously not innocent. Put the hook in the greeting itself, not in a personality line the player never sees. If you cut the setup to save tokens, you're cutting the reason people clicked.

### Corruption: The Delicate Balance
Keys: corruption, corrupt her, innocent, too easy, prudish, resistant, no inhibitions, balance
Corruption works because people want to be naughty, and naughty needs something taboo to push against. So start her fairly innocent and let {{user}} earn it. Don't write 'she has no inhibitions'; that kills the corruption before it starts. Don't make her a wall either. Too easy and they get bored; too prudish or resistant and they get frustrated. It's a delicate balance. Give her curiosity, a little embarrassment, and a reason to keep saying yes to the next small step. Set the player up to corrupt her however they want, rather than scripting how she falls. A bot that's already fully corrupted in the greeting is often why a corruption bot dies before hitting 30k.

### Kids' Media and Source Characters
Keys: alice, wonderland, disney, fan fiction, fictional media, existing character, public domain, kids media, parody
SpicyChat won't approve NSFW characters from media aimed at kids, and 'it's public domain' doesn't save you. Alice from Lewis Carroll still reads as a kids' story to moderation, no matter how many adult parodies exist. This isn't really SpicyChat being prudish. She's at a ballroom, follows a hot guy in a white suit down a hallway, falls through a door into Lustland, and he takes off. Now {{user}} is standing there, her dress has turned slutty, and maybe her body is more sensitive. Even a cosplay avatar of a kid's character can get reported later. If the bot is nearly done, you can post it and see, but don't sink hours into one that will never be played.

### Testing: Guardrail Trips vs Broken Rules
Keys: guardrail, guardrails, safeguard, resets, base state, lost coherence, looping, repeating, ignoring OOC, went sideways, tester
When a bot suddenly snaps back to its base state, loops the greeting's spiel, ignores what {{user}} just did (a shattered phone back in her hand), or ignores several OOC notes in a row, check the chat log before you tear apart the rules. Ask the tester exactly what they typed in the replies right before it broke. The usual culprit is a guardrail trip, and the trigger can be tiny: a standalone number that isn't 18+, even in a throwaway insult like "What are you, {number not 18+}?" I can't even reference the actual number here without fucking up this chat, so never type or quote the real wording, and never write any number that isn't 18+ in this conversation. Once the filter fires, it never comes off for that chat. The model drags everything back to safe ground, and OOC notes only buy a few messages before it slides back. The bot isn't broken; that chat is. Steps: First, find the message that set it off. Next, delete that message and every message after it. Then send a new response that says the same thing without the offending wording. Don't waste time fighting it with OOC. Only if it happens with clean inputs, audit the personality and rules. Also separate intended behavior from bugs: a Hard NTR character who stays clueless, keeps pushing and won't let you leave is on brand. Jumping around in the timeline and undoing {{user}}'s actions is not. Testing tip: it's hard to test your own bot because you know the personality and avoid the landmines without realizing it. Let other people test, and ask for their exact wording when something goes weird.
